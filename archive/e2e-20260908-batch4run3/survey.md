# World Models for Games: A Survey

## Abstract
This survey covers 4 themes over 12 citation-ready studies, spanning Procedural Content Generation Mechanisms to Latent Architectures for World Representation. Every claim carries the tag of the paper whose evidence supports it. Grandmaster level in StarCraft II using multi-agent reinforcement learning: The Holy Grail for AI is to surpass the best human players in complex games such as StarCraft (Blizzard Entertainment, 1998), a sci-fi military-strat [paper:10.1038/s41586-019-1724-z]. DIFFUSION MODELS ARE REAL-TIME GAME ENGINES: We present GameNGen, the first game engine powered entirely by a neural model that also enables real-time interaction with a complex environment over [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2408.14837]. Mastering Atari, Go, chess and shogi by planning with a learned model addresses the challenge of constructing agents with planning capabilities, building on the huge success that tree-based planning methods have enjoyed in domains such as chess and Go [paper:10.1038/s41586-020-03051-4].

## Introduction
Game intelligence increasingly rests on models that learn how a world evolves rather than on hand-written rules alone; whether such models can support planning, generation, and fair evaluation is the question this survey addresses. Each theme that follows gathers the evidence for one capability and states where that evidence stops.

Procedural Content Generation Mechanisms surveys examines how world models integrate procedural content generation algorithms to dynamically create game levels, assets, and environments, enhancing replayability and adaptability within gaming simulations. Benchmark Datasets for World Model Evaluation collects the work on reviews the standardized datasets, evaluation metrics, and protocols used to assess the performance, sample efficiency, and generalization capabilities of world models in various game environments. Internal Simulation and Autonomous Agent Dynamics follows the evidence for focuses on the internal predictive models used for forward simulation and the behavior of autonomous agents operating within these simulated worlds, including planning, decision-making, and interaction dynamics. Latent Architectures for World Representation maps what is known about details the neural network architectures and latent space designs employed to encode, store, and reconstruct world states, emphasizing efficiency, scalability, and semantic meaningfulness in representation learning.

## Procedural Content Generation Mechanisms

Procedural Content Generation Mechanisms focuses on Examines how world models integrate procedural content generation algorithms to dynamically create game levels, assets, and environments, enhancing replayability and adaptability within gaming simulations.

Framed as procedural content generation mechanisms, world models offer a learned pathway for creating game levels, assets, and environments, making generative simulation a natural meeting point of the surveyed work. Diffusion Models Are Real-Time Game Engines [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2408.14837] presents GameNGen, the first game engine powered entirely by a neural model that also enables real-time interaction with a complex environment over long trajectories at high quality. When trained on the classic game DOOM, GameNGen demonstrates that a complex playable environment can be dynamically generated by a neural model in real time, rather than executed by hand-coded engine logic [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2408.14837]. Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2301.04104] tackles the fundamental challenge of developing a general algorithm that learns to solve tasks across a wide range of applications, supplying the generality on which adaptable, cross-domain content generation depends. DriveDreamer [paper:10.1007/978-3-031-73195-2_4] extends the same mechanism to real-world settings, arguing that an established world model holds immense potential for generation through its capacity for comprehending driving environments.

![Publication years of the selected representative papers.](publication_timeline)

At the mechanism level, a world model learns a general representation of the environment and predicts future environment states based on historical observations and ego actions, providing the predictive substrate from which new content and situations can be synthesized [paper:10.1007/978-3-031-73195-2_4]. GameNGen instantiates this substrate for games by training a diffusion model on the classic game DOOM, after which the engine sustains high-quality, real-time interaction over long trajectories [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2408.14837]. Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models contributes a training-side mechanism, pursuing a single general algorithm that learns to solve tasks across a wide range of applications, motivated by the brittleness of applying reinforcement learning to new problems. [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2301.04104].

Directly compared, GameNGen and DriveDreamer share the principle of predicting or generating future environment states from interaction history, but they diverge in deployment target: real-time interactive gaming on one side and real-world driving simulation on the other [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2408.14837] [paper:10.1007/978-3-031-73195-2_4]. Relative to the breadth pursued by Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models, GameNGen concentrates on reproducing one complex environment over long trajectories at high quality, illustrating a breadth-versus-fidelity split in how world models implement generative content [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2301.04104] [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2408.14837].

These mechanisms inherit clear constraints: in Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models, brittleness poses a bottleneck in applying reinforcement learning to new problems and also limits the applicability of reinforcement learning to computationally expensive models or tasks where tuning is prohibitive [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2301.04104]. On the data side, DriveDreamer reports that prior approaches fall short primarily due to their task-specific optimization and reliance on limited human-annotated training data, a constraint that any dynamically generating world model for content creation must confront [paper:10.1007/978-3-031-73195-2_4].

## Benchmark Datasets for World Model Evaluation

Benchmark Datasets for World Model Evaluation focuses on Reviews the standardized datasets, evaluation metrics, and protocols used to assess the performance, sample efficiency, and generalization capabilities of world models in various game environments.

Standardized evaluation of world models in game environments draws on complementary testbeds spanning generative interactive environments, social deduction benchmarks, and learned-model planning suites. Genie [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2402.15391] is the first generative interactive environment trained in an unsupervised manner from unlabelled Internet videos, and it can be prompted to generate an endless variety of action-controllable virtual environments. AvalonBench provides a structured evaluation setting for Large Language Model agents in the social deduction game Resistance Avalon, where agents must make informed decisions while also handling deception and collaboration [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2310.05036]. Mastering Atari, Go, chess and shogi by planning with a learned model contributes agents with planning capabilities, extending the huge success of tree-based planning methods from domains such as chess and Go to Atari, Go, chess and shogi [paper:10.1038/s41586-020-03051-4].

Mastering Atari, Go, chess and shogi by planning with a learned model centers on planning with a learned model, an approach motivated by the long-standing challenge of constructing agents with planning capabilities in the pursuit of artificial intelligence [paper:10.1038/s41586-020-03051-4]. Genie is trained unsupervised from unlabelled Internet videos, and hypothetical reasoning in training using traversable latent spaces has been noted to allow sim-to-real transfer of video world models conditioned on actions [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2402.15391]. AvalonBench structures play of Resistance Avalon so that agents must navigate complex dynamics involving deception and collaboration with other agents, providing a structured context for evaluating strategic social deduction behavior [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2310.05036].

Whereas tree-based planning with a learned model is evaluated in challenging domains such as chess, Go, shogi and Atari, AvalonBench evaluates agents in the social deduction game Resistance Avalon, part of a broader family of social deduction games such as Werewolf, Chameleon, Avalon, and Jubensha that exemplify navigating deception and collaboration [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2310.05036]. Genie differs from these fixed game benchmarks by synthesizing a wide range of action-controllable 2D worlds, having been trained in an unsupervised manner on unlabelled Internet videos, and it was among the first models to move towards an interactive environment setting rather than pure video generation. [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2402.15391].

A key limitation is that other agents in social deduction scenarios limit the effectiveness of current approaches in settings that require theory of mind or strategic social deduction, which AvalonBench revisits in the context of Avalon as a social deduction game [paper:10.48550/arxiv.2310.05036]. Constructing agents with planning capabilities has long been one of the main challenges in the pursuit of artificial intelligence, indicating that evaluation of learned-model planning in domains such as Atari, Go, chess and shogi remains an ongoing challenge [paper:10.1038/s41586-020-03051-4].
